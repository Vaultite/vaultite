# Abstract: the card index as partner

Submitted 2026-01-15 for the open panel on research infrastructures. Accepted 2026-02-02. Limit 250 words.

## Title

The card index as partner: Luhmann, Otlet and the infrastructure of scholarly thought

## Abstract

Sociologists of science have long studied the inscriptions that circulate through laboratories [@latour1986], but have paid less attention to the private inscription systems that researchers keep for themselves. This paper treats the note system as infrastructure in Star's sense [@star1999]: embedded in practice, learned as part of membership, and visible mainly when it breaks. It compares two paper machines of the twentieth century. Paul Otlet's documentation project broke knowledge into units on standard cards so that it could be classified universally [@otlet1934]. Niklas Luhmann's card index, by contrast, gave each card a fixed address and linked it to others, aiming not at a universal order but at a private partner able to surprise its author [@luhmann1981]. Read together, they mark two answers to the problem of scholarly abundance that @blair2010 traces back to the sixteenth century: classify everything, or connect enough. The paper argues that the second answer, now built into digital note-taking tools, changes what kinds of questions a researcher can find their way back to, and closes with questions for the study of shared research vaults as boundary objects [@star1989].
