# Grant proposal: infrastructures of attention

Draft for the Northfield University Humanities Research Fund, standard grant. Deadline 2027-02-15. Duration 24 months.

## Summary

Researchers increasingly do their thinking inside personal note systems: card indexes, notebooks and now linked digital vaults. This project studies such systems as research infrastructure, combining archival work on historical paper machines with interviews and observation of present-day researchers. It asks how the structure of a note system (addressing, linking, retrieval) shapes what researchers notice and write.

## Background

Historians have shown that scholarly note-taking has a long history of technical invention [@blair2010; @krajewski2011], and Luhmann's account of his card index has become a reference point well beyond sociology [@luhmann1981; @ahrens2017]. Philosophers and cognitive scientists have argued that external records can be part of cognition itself [@clark1998; @hutchins1995]. Yet no study has followed note systems as infrastructure, in the sense of embedded, standardised and mostly invisible arrangements of work [@star1999; @bowker1999].

## Aims

1. Reconstruct the design principles of three historical note systems: Otlet's documentation cards [@otlet1934], Luhmann's index [@schmidt2016] and the Northfield card collection (see [[Northfield card index]]).
2. Observe how twenty researchers in three disciplines use their present note systems over one term, with a focus on retrieval.
3. Produce a framework for describing note systems as infrastructure, tested in a graduate course ([[Syllabus]]).

## Methods

- Archival: catalogue and photograph the Northfield cards; compare numbering and linking schemes.
- Ethnographic: think-aloud sessions while writing, plus a log of what each participant finds again and how [@latour1986].
- Analytic: map links in each system as a network, borrowing from citation analysis [@price1965].
