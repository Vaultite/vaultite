## Summary

A practical book that turned Luhmann's index into a method for students and writers. Ahrens separates fleeting notes, literature notes and permanent notes, and argues that the value of the system lies less in the archive than in the habits it forces: writing in your own words, deciding where a note connects, and letting the topic of a piece of writing emerge from what has accumulated.

## Notes

- Strongest claim: writing is not the last step of research but the medium in which it happens; notes are where the writing starts.
- Literature notes are short and in your own words, with the reference kept separately; the reference manager holds the bibliographic details.
- Permanent notes should be understandable without their source, one idea each, and linked on creation.
- Leans on psychology for support (the generation effect, retrieval practice); some of it is stated more strongly than the studies allow.
- Little history: Luhmann appears as an exemplary individual rather than as part of a tradition of paper technologies.
