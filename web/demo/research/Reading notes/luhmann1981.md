## Summary

A short essay in which Luhmann describes how he built and used the card index behind his writing. He presents the index as a communication partner: a system with enough internal structure that consulting it produces results its author did not put in deliberately. The essay mixes practical instructions with a systems-theoretical argument about what it takes for any partner to be able to surprise us.

## Notes

- Fixed positions: each card gets a number that never changes, so it can always be referred to; a card that continues another is filed right after it and numbered as a branch.
- Links: cards refer to other cards by number, across the whole collection, which is what lets distant topics meet.
- The register is deliberately sparse: a few entry points per keyword, from which the links do the rest.
- The index has to be internally complex and partly forgotten by its author; otherwise it only gives back what was put in.
- Writing for the index means restating ideas, not copying them, which is the part later popularisers stress most [[ahrens2017]].
