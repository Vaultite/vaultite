## Summary

An ethnography of navigation aboard a US Navy ship, used to argue that cognition is a property of systems of people and artefacts rather than of individual minds. Fixing the ship's position is a computation carried out by bearing takers, plotters, charts and instruments, with no single participant holding the whole of it.

## Notes

- The unit of analysis is the functional system, not the person; representations move between media (spoken bearings, logbook entries, lines on a chart).
- Tools do not amplify individual cognition so much as change the task: the chart turns a calculation into a drawing.
- History matters: navigation techniques carry the solutions of earlier generations, which makes them a kind of crystallised knowledge.
- Learning happens through participation in the system, often at its edges, before a newcomer understands the whole.
