## Summary

A history of how early modern European scholars coped with the flood of books that print made possible. Blair studies reference genres (florilegia, encyclopaedias, bibliographies) and the note-taking practices behind them, showing that information overload was a perceived problem long before computers and that many of our tools for it are old.

## Notes

- Four operations organise the book: storing, sorting, selecting and summarising.
- Compilers relied on secretaries and amanuenses; note-taking was often collective work, which complicates the image of the solitary scholar.
- Slips cut and pasted into new orders were common; Gessner is a key figure here too.
- Readers worried about the same things we do: too much to read, the loss of deep reading, dependence on digests.
- Excellent appendix-like discussion of indexes and their making.
